# The problem, in my own words

**Who:** Nadia Comeau, CEO of Sandbar Scheduling, who signs off on the shows and answers to her co-founder Sam. Owen Gallant (sales) has most of the booth conversations. Erin Raman (operations, Tuesdays and Thursdays) cleans up afterwards.

**What they experience:** After a show, what people said is spread across badge scans, business cards, Owen's voice notes and his flight list. Nobody owns the week after. Owen works from his list, from his own mailbox, at night between renewals. Erin sends a template from hello without knowing who Owen wrote to, and holds back cards marked "demo" for Owen without telling him. Nadia's cards stay in her blazer, and her promises stay in her head.

**The impact:** A show costs about $9,000, and Nadia can't tell Sam what it brought in. In Toronto, 49 of 80 real conversations never received an email: 46 had gone nowhere by day 30, and 3 booked or started a trial on their own. Of the 16 people who asked for a demo, pricing, a trial or a call, 9 received nothing and 1 received only the template. Owen's emails went out 13 to 17 days after the show. All 6 people with a promise he wrote to personally went on to book a demo or start a trial, but he ran out of nights after 8 emails. Yvonne, who said "book me a demo", was never written to. Jen got two emails, and 5 template emails went to people who never talked to us. Erin spent about 10 hours, and her invoices and inbox slid. One new clinic is worth about $2,400 a year, so two or three would pay for a show.

**How often:** Four two-day shows a year. Montreal's scan export was never downloaded, so that show's scans are gone.

**The root cause:** It looks like slow follow-up, but follow-up is slow because nobody owns that week, the records are never joined, and nobody can see what anyone else did. So promises drop out at each hand-off. And even a perfect list wouldn't fix it alone: Owen says he won't write 80 emails, so the drafts are missing too.

**The outcome they want:** By Erin's first day in after a show, every real conversation is one person, with a priority, a next step and a draft from whoever had the conversation that does what was promised. Nobody who asked to be left alone gets one, and nobody gets two. At day 30, every conversation is in one of four piles with names: booked a demo, started a trial, written to with nothing from it, or never written to. Nadia uses those piles, side by side across shows, to decide which show to drop next year. Erin's test: a month after a show, "never written to" is close to empty.
